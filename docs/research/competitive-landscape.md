Research date: 14 November 2026. Prices are in the vendor's own currency and come from vendor pages unless stated.

## Headline findings

1. **The NZ DIY field is thinner than it looks.** Keyhook now sells to property managers, Renti serves renters and property managers, and Experian Tenancy (formerly illion Tenancy) closes on 4 December 2026. myRent is the one established product aimed squarely at self-managing landlords.
2. **Nobody in NZ sells on-demand physical tasks to DIY landlords.** OpenRent (UK) and Showdigs (US) prove the model with public per-task prices. Australian attempts at flat-fee, remote management (:Different, Lodge) failed.
3. **The "instant expert" need is real and measurable.** Roughly 32,000 dispute applications were made in the last 12 months, 84% by landlords, and 58% involved rent arrears. Tenants' top issues are bond refunds, compensation, exemplary damages, Healthy Homes and maintenance.
4. **A 0-100 renter score is the highest-risk element of the concept.** Snug's 0-100 Match Score, 2Apply and SafeRent all drew regulatory or legal action. South Australia already bans ratings that reflect higher rent offers.
5. **Nelson is a test market, not a business.** Nelson and Tasman hold about 7,000 active bonds, of which perhaps 3,100 are self-managed (derived).
6. **The free tier covers most of the target segment.** Monetisation therefore has to come from transactions (listings, checks, physical tasks), which is how OpenRent and TurboTenant work.

## A. NZ self-management and DIY landlord tools

### myRent (myrent.co.nz)

- **What it is:** Online self-management platform launched 1 May 2018, covering tenant finding, rent collection from multiple tenants and maintenance tracking (https://www.goodreturns.co.nz/article/976513574/lightening-landlords-workloads.html).
- **Price (snippets only, confirm live):** about NZ$19-20 per property per month†, with an older FAQ figure of $17†. Hands-Free Rent Collection is an add-on at about +$14 per property per month†. First two months free† (https://www.myrent.co.nz/management/pricing, https://www.myrent.co.nz/hands-free-rent-collection).
- **Tenant checks:** NZ$35 and NZ$55 tiers† (https://www.myrent.co.nz/tenant-checks). A reviewer confirms paying $55 for the full report (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/myrent.co.nz?stars=1&stars=2&stars=3).
- **Tenant cost:** tenants pay nothing unless they pay rent by card, which carries a merchant fee†.
- **Reputation:** Trustpilot 4.8 from 2,981 reviews (88% five-star, 429 in the last 12 months). Praise centres on ease of use, everything in one place, responsive support, and listing reach on myRent and Trade Me (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/myrent.co.nz).
- **Complaints (20 low-star reviews, Nov 2024 to Oct 2026):**
  - Tenant checks are the most common problem: stalled for days, incomplete, built on partial name matches, self-reported income and passport-number-only ID. Two landlords say the delay cost them the applicant.
  - Applicants got no status updates after acceptance; one went elsewhere.
  - The Healthy Homes compliance form was hard to complete; another landlord felt the platform checking their uploads was overreach.
  - Unexpected charges, no bank-account payment for fees, and no export of listing and message records for insurance.
  - Tenant-side: must register to apply; one found the platform confusing.
- **Not found:** any on-demand physical service, any AI feature. Bank feeds, bond lodgement integration, notice generation and tax reports are UNVERIFIED.

### Keyhook (keyhook.com)

- **Now a property-manager product.** The site pitches "All-in-One Property Management, Powered by AI" with trust account reconciliation, owner approval workflows and owner, tenant and supplier portals (https://www.keyhook.com). The App Store listing says "Built for property managers" (https://apps.apple.com/nz/app/keyhook/id1570942532).
- **Price:** quote only, based on portfolio size and features (https://www.keyhook.com/pricing).
- **Features:** syndication to 10+ platforms, ID verification with liveness checks, combined credit, reference and income assessment, maintenance with photo or video, offline inspection app, bank feeds with automated payment matching, AI invoice processing.
- **AI:** "Tama", described in a sponsored article as an assistant giving free, real-time advice based on local law to property managers, landlords and tenants (https://nzherald.co.nz/sponsored-stories/how-a-kiwi-innovation-is-disrupting-a-15-billion-industry/QNABGYVR2VB53J7RQHKATC4HJI, 11 Nov 2024). This is a vendor claim.
- **Scale:** "25,000+ active users" and "4.9 app rating" are vendor claims. The NZ App Store shows 4.0 from 10 ratings, with a bug complaint. Trustpilot has one review (1 star, Aug 2026) saying bonds and agreements could not be reliably lodged (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/keyhook.com).
- **UNVERIFIED:** whether the original free offering for self-managing landlords still exists.

### Renti (renti.co)

- **Positioning:** "for renters and property managers". The homepage does not address private landlords (https://www.renti.co).
- **Scale (vendor claims):** 800K+ users, 280K+ properties, 1.6M+ applications.
- **Renti Rewards:** renters earn points for paying rent by card, with rent protection, insurance access and discounts; it "works with any NZ landlord or property manager".
- **Price:** not published. Payment terms refer to fees for "the Agency and or Private Landlord" (https://site2.renti.co/payment-terms-and-conditions)†.
- **Why it matters:** Renti already holds a large base of renter application profiles on the agent-managed side of the market. Pool would be competing with that installed base whenever a renter applies to both managed and self-managed properties.

### Landlord Studio (landlordstudio.com)

- **Price (US page, USD):** Go free for up to 3 units; Pro $15/month; Pro Plus $35/month; $1.20 per extra unit beyond three (https://www.landlordstudio.com/pricing).
- **Features:** income and expense tracking, receipt scanner, online rent collection, automated late fees and reminders, tenant portal, maintenance tracking, listings and tenant-paid screening (US). Pro adds bank feeds, Xero integration, AI-assisted bookkeeping and 20+ reports.
- **Reviews:** Trustpilot has only 4 reviews (3.0); complaints are account suspension without appeal and a limited trial (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/landlordstudio.com).
- **UNVERIFIED:** NZ pricing in NZD, which features work in NZ, and the NZ founding story (the About page names co-founders Charles Chan and Logan Ransley but not a location).

### Listing portals

- **realestate.co.nz:** the rentals page states it "now allows rental listings from private landlords" (https://www.realestate.co.nz/residential/rental). Price UNVERIFIED.
- **Trade Me Property:** fees for private rental listings are UNVERIFIED (help centre blocked). myRent reviewers confirm listings reach Trade Me through myRent.
- **Rent must be stated:** NZ landlords must state the rent in any advertisement and cannot invite bids (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/rent-bond-and-bills/rent/charging-rent/). Pool's "fixed prices" is therefore the legal baseline, not a differentiator, unless Pool also refuses voluntary over-asking offers.

### What NZ DIY tools do not do

- No on-demand viewings, inspections or check-in services.
- No conversational maintenance triage or tradie dispatch.
- No visible guardrails that stop a landlord making an unlawful move (wrong notice period, late bond, missing statements).
- Screening is the weakest part of the incumbent's product, and one bureau-backed provider is leaving the market.

**What the pro tools do that DIY tools lack**

- **Money:** trust accounting with audit-ready reconciliation, or direct-to-owner payment that removes the trust account altogether.
- **Maintenance:** conversational triage, tradie marketplaces with matching, contractor compliance tracking, automated chasing and invoice-to-job matching.
- **Inspections:** routine workflows with 360° photos and AI-written reports.
- **Compliance:** legislation monitoring and Healthy Homes tracking.
- **Arrears and comms:** automated arrears sequences and a unified inbox.
- **Bond:** NZ landlords using integrated property management software can lodge, top up and refund bonds from that software (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/rent-bond-and-bills/bond/using-property-management-software-to-complete-bond-transactions/). **Portal constraints in Australia**

- Domain ended direct listings by self-managed landlords from January 2021 (https://eliteagent.com/?p=185702).
- realestate.com.au launched direct rental listings for self-managed landlords in August 2020 (https://eliteagent.com/rea-launch-new-tools-for-self-managed-landlords/). Its customer page still quotes $120-200 including GST (https://customer.realestate.com.au/residential/self-managed-landlords/). Whether this is still purchasable in 2026 is UNVERIFIED.
- In practice DIY platforms hold an agent licence and list on the landlord's behalf, charging about A$199-205 per listing.

**Lessons**

- The surviving Australian players charge a one-off listing fee of about A$200 and convert to A$29-36 per month. Loop's NZ$25 sits just under that band.
- Flat-fee full management at scale has not worked. Remote or offshore delivery degraded the physical tasks, which is exactly the part Loop proposes to sell.

### What renters like

- **Storing documents and references once, with status updates.** This is what Snug's positive reviews praise (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/snug.com).
- **Rent reporting that builds credit.** CreditLadder (UK) scores 4.8 from 2,536 reviews (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/creditladder.co.uk).
- **Landlord-rating sites are legally fragile.** NZ's "What Does My Landlord Own?" was taken down in June 2023 after a privacy finding (https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/496656/property-tracking-website-that-showed-what-landlords-own-shut-for-breaching-privacy-act).

### NZ law that shapes Pool

- **Staged collection.** Privacy Commissioner guidance (https://www.privacy.org.nz/resources-and-learning/a-z-topics/rental-guidance-for-landlords/):
  - Viewing: name and contact details only.
  - Application: ID, 18+, number of occupants, referee contacts, consent for checks, pets and smoking where relevant.
  - Preferred applicant: credit or criminal check, plus one form of evidence of ability to pay, chosen by the tenant.
  - Never: Human Rights Act characteristics (including employment status, age, family status), transaction-level bank statements, employment history, social media URLs.
- **Credit checks** should only be run when "actively negotiating an offer".
- **Blacklists** are called "problematic" and may breach several privacy principles.
- **Retention:** unsuccessful applicants' information should not be kept beyond a short period.
- **Newer rules†:** IPP 3A (notify people when collecting about them indirectly) from 1 May 2026 (https://www.privacy.org.nz/resources-and-learning/a-z-topics/ipp3a/); the Biometric Processing Privacy Code applies to face-match ID (https://minterellison.co.nz/insights/biometric-processing-privacy-code-now-in-force).
- **Whether positive rent-payment data can go on NZ credit files** is UNVERIFIED.

### 2. Top 10 unmet needs

1. **Not knowing the rules until the Tribunal explains them.** Exemplary damages feature in about 9% of applications, and "I was inexperienced" is not accepted as an excuse (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/about-tenancy-services/news/landlords-fined-for-tenancy-breaches-across-34-properties/). No NZ DIY tool was found that blocks an unlawful step before it happens.
2. **Arrears handling.** Rent arrears are in 58.5% of applications, almost all landlord-filed (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/about-tenancy-services/data-and-statistics/). The incumbent charges extra for rent collection.
3. **Bond handling at both ends.** Bond refunds are in 42.6% of applications, and "bond not lodged" appears in about 11% of tenant applications (same source).
4. **Slow, low-quality tenant checks.** This is myRent's most common complaint, with landlords losing applicants to the delay (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/myrent.co.nz?stars=1&stars=2&stars=3). Experian Tenancy leaves the market on 4 December 2026 (https://www.experian.co.nz/lp/tenancy-message).
5. **Nobody to do the physical work.** No NZ DIY tool sells viewings or inspections. Insurers may require proof of regular inspections (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/maintenance-and-inspections/inspections/). OpenRent shows the demand (https://www.openrent.co.uk/online-letting-agent-pricing-and-fees).
6. **Maintenance that tenants are afraid to request.** Healthy Homes and failure-to-maintain each appear in over a quarter of tenant applications; 91.4% of surveyed NZ renters had never used the 14-day notice (https://waateanews.com/?p=166507).
7. **Healthy Homes paperwork.** myRent reviewers found the compliance form hard; penalties reach $7,200 (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/healthy-homes/healthy-homes-compliance/).
8. **Application overload and silence for renters.** 60% are uncomfortable with what is collected (https://www.choice.com.au/data-protection-and-privacy/data-collection-and-use/how-your-data-is-used/articles/choice-renttech-report-release); myRent applicants report no updates after acceptance.
9. **A human who answers.** No phone or unreachable support is the most repeated complaint across RentBetter, Cubbi, :Different, OpenRent and TurboTenant (https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/rentbetter, https://www.trustpilot.com/review/openrent.co.uk). myRent's responsive support is the bar to beat.
10. **Evidence and records.** The regulator tells landlords to "document everything" (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/about-tenancy-services/news/proactive-compliance-key-lessons-for-landlords-from-dunedin-inspections/); a myRent reviewer could not export listing and message records for an insurer.

### 5. Recommended differentiators

1. **Guardrails, not guidance.** Encode the Residential Tenancies Act as rules the product enforces: bond lodged inside 23 working days, compliance statements attached before an agreement can be sent, inspection notices that cannot be issued with under 48 hours' notice or more often than every 4 weeks, termination notices with the correct period for the tenancy type. Show the rule and its source at the moment it applies.
2. **Arrears and bond on autopilot, with a Tribunal-ready file.** These two issues drive most disputes. Detect a missed payment from the bank feed, send the right notice at the right time, and assemble the evidence pack automatically.
3. **Physical tasks with a public price list and an auditable report.** Viewings, entry and exit inspections and routine inspections, each with a fixed price, a standard checklist, time-stamped photos and an AI-drafted report the landlord approves.
4. **A maintenance front door tenants are not afraid to use.** No-login chat, troubleshooting first, a visible status for the tenant, a one-tap approval for the landlord, and a Healthy Homes flag when the issue is a compliance matter.
5. **Pool as a verified profile, not a score.** Replace the 0-100 number with verified facts (identity, ability to pay evidenced by a method the renter chooses, references confirmed). Follow the Privacy Commissioner's stages exactly, let the renter control and revoke sharing, never charge the renter, never let an offer above asking affect anything, and delete on a timer. If a number is kept, publish every factor.
6. **Fast, consented screening.** The incumbent is slow here and a bureau-backed provider is exiting. Run checks only at the preferred-applicant stage, show the applicant what was found, and give both sides live status.
7. **No client money.** Rent goes directly to the landlord's account and is reconciled from the bank feed. Loop bills its own fee separately.
8. **A person who answers.** Named, local, reachable support, with escalation to a tenancy expert, is the gap every competitor's reviews point to.
9. **Monetise transactions, since the subscription will be free for most.** Listings, checks, physical tasks, insurance and utilities referrals should carry the model; test this before relying on the NZ$25 tier.
10. **A 30-minute compliant start.** Import or audit an existing agreement, flag what is missing, lodge the bond, schedule the first inspection and set up rent tracking in one guided flow. This is the moment a first-time landlord feels like an expert.
