# Australian residential tenancy law - rule-pack research

Research date: **14 November 2026**. Scope: private residential tenancies (general tenancies / standard leases). Rooming houses, caravan and residential parks, social housing and purpose-built student accommodation are out of scope unless noted.

## How to read this file

- Every state table uses the same columns: `Rule key | Value | Act & section | In force since | Source URL | Confidence | Notes`.
- Rule keys are identical across states so they map onto one schema. A key that does not apply in a state is still listed with the value `n/a` or `none`.
- **Confidence**
  - `primary-verified` - read during this research on the regulator, government or legislation page given in the Source URL column.
  - `secondary-verified` - read on a non-government source, or inferred from a primary page that does not state the point outright.
  - `unverified` - from prior knowledge, not confirmed this session. **Do not ship as a hard block without checking.**
- Section numbers marked with a dagger (†) come from prior knowledge, not from the cited page. The value in the row can still be primary-verified while its section number is not.
- Units are kept as the regulator states them. Queensland uses months ("2 months"), most other states use days, the ACT uses weeks. Store value and unit separately; do not convert 2 months to 60 days.

## Research limits (read before coding)

1. The web-search quota for the session ran out part-way through. Everything after that was done by opening regulator sites directly, so "nothing found" for an upcoming reform means "not listed on the regulator's own pages", not "confirmed absent".
2. State legislation sites could not be read (NSW legislation site sits behind a bot check, AustLII returned 403). Section numbers therefore come mostly from regulator pages. Rows without a section number were not confirmed against the Act.
3. Some regulator pages were read through a summarising fetch tool rather than verbatim. Numbers from those pages were cross-checked where a second page existed.
4. Three conflicts or surprises are flagged in "Conflicts and unverified items" at the end. The Victorian one affects a hard-block rule.

The CAV notice-to-vacate page, updated 2 Nov 2026, still carries a paragraph saying a no-reason notice is possible at the end of the first fixed term, although its reasons table lists no such ground. The paragraph looks like leftover pre-reform text. This file treats the ground as abolished. Confirm against the current Residential Tenancies Act 1997 (former s 91ZZD) before hard-blocking.

3. **WA and NT still allow without-grounds termination** (WA 60 days periodic and 30 days at fixed-term end; NT 60 days for both). QLD and TAS removed it for periodic tenancies only: the end of a fixed term is still a valid reason there.

5. **VIC bond lodgement deadline.** CAV now says 14 days; older material said 10 business days. Coded as 14 days per CAV.

## 13. Notes for the rules engine

- **Unit handling.** Notice periods come in hours, days, business days, working days, weeks and months. Keep the unit with the number.
- **Windows, not just minimums.** WA inspections (7 to 14 days), SA inspections (7 to 28 days) and TAS end-of-fixed-term notices (42 to 60 days before the end date) have a maximum lead time as well.
- **Day counting differs.** NSW: calendar days, day of service excluded, add 7 working days if posted. QLD: exclude the day of giving; a period ending on a weekend or public holiday rolls forward. VIC entry notices: only by post or personal delivery between 8am and 6pm. Model service method per state.
- **Two-step arrears flows.** QLD, WA, SA, ACT and NT need a remedy notice before any termination step; NSW, VIC and TAS use a single notice that payment can void.
- **Evidence attachments are validity conditions** in NSW, VIC and SA for sale, move-in, renovation and demolition grounds. A notice without the listed document is invalid, so the engine should block issue until the document is attached.
- **Re-letting exclusions** (NSW 4 weeks to 12 months; VIC, QLD, SA 6 months) should block a new listing for the property.
- **Advertised rent is a ceiling in VIC and QLD.** Elsewhere the platform must never prompt for a higher offer but may record one made voluntarily.
- **Platform fees.** VIC bans any application or rent-payment fee charged to renters, including by third-party platforms. NSW, SA, WA and QLD require a fee-free or low-cost method alongside any paid one, and NSW bars requiring a particular app.
- **Prescribed application forms** apply in VIC, QLD and SA; the intake form must not ask for anything beyond them.
- **Queensland rent-increase history is per property**, surviving changes of tenant, agent and owner.
